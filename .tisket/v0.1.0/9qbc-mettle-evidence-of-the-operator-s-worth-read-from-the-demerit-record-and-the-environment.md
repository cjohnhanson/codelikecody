Build mettle: the tool that shows the power and the importance of the human operator, with evidence for two readers. The operator reads it as recognition: what only you brought, accumulating over time. The boss reads it as legible value: what this person caught, taught, and built that the model and the harness did not. The register is human and warm, not forensic. The deliverable is closer to a brag document with receipts than to an audit.

The substrate is three books. The flow book is the demerit store: the corrections, one event per moment of human judgment. The stock book is the standing operator artifacts: rules files, memory, skills, hooks, fixtures, and the code history, with provenance from git. The attribution book decomposes agent transcripts into operator spans, harness spans, and model spans, over the claude-code protocol crate, with gaff events as the consumption signal. A character share is a floor, not a verdict. The strong evidence is the ablation: the same task with a bare harness and with the operator's full stock, diffed missouri-style; the delta is the operator, measured.

The group language turns human: 'only you' (no rule can hold it yet), 'teachable' (it occurs again; a rule can take it), 'taught' (a rule runs it now). demerit stays a lean log and never becomes a platform; mettle reads demerit and the environment, and writes the story.

Open problem, the hardest one: the boss-reader needs outcome language ('caught a premature deploy claim'), and the record holds tool language ('severity 1, premature-done'). What translation is honest without a human editing every word? Second open problem: what makes a body of per-task evidence add up to a career-level claim instead of a pile of anecdotes?

## 2026-08-13 — the ablation feature, made concrete by the user (v2 centerpiece)

The user defined the strongest form of the prove book: take a real session, extract the intent and the achieved end state, strip the operator stock (memories, CLAUDE.md, skills, hooks) into a vanilla environment, and re-run the same intent with an intentionally-layperson driver: an LLM that gets the intent in plain words and either plays a strict non-expert or is a model without the technical knowledge. The side-by-side answers 'what did I bring that a non-expert could not', in turns, in time, or in convergence.

Design positions from the discussion: three conditions (expert baseline, instructed layperson under a strict outcome-only protocol, weaker model), because a persona leaks knowledge and a weak model conflates capability with expertise; the intent brief must hold outcome language only, because an expert-written brief smuggles expertise; the environment strip uses the family's worktree isolation; convergence is acceptance criteria extracted from the real session plus a fixed budget, never a byte diff against the expert's end state. ## 2026-08-13 — the v2 capture architecture, set by the user

Two design calls from the user. First: capture every human prompt, with no judgment at capture time, and pull the corrections out with a separate pass over the record. This resolves the passive-versus-explicit divergence from the clean-room round: capture is total, so false negatives cannot exist, and extraction is re-runnable as classifiers improve. Second: the storage backend is pluggable, with duckdb as an option beside markdown-in-git.

The architecture in family idiom: capture is gaff-shaped (per-harness adapters normalize every prompt into one envelope over a stdin contract); storage is belmont-shaped (a Store trait; markdown-in-git canonical for the evidence chain, duckdb as a full backend that can serve as a materialized index; the derived database is always rebuildable from the canonical record); extraction is a pluggable Extractor trait over the raw log (mechanical first, LLM-assisted as a separate labeled implementation; derived moments live beside the raw record and never replace it); the instruments (report, attribution, replay) read through the Store trait. Recommended composition, open to redirection: one record tool owns capture, storage, and extraction, and mettle stays the reading instrument. The architecture-eval findings (schema contract, attribute seam, ADRs, shared convention constants) become the ADR backlog of this design.

## 2026-08-13 — the product in one sentence, from the user

The null hypothesis is that the operator's expertise explains no variance in outcomes. The tool helps a person gather the data and run the experiments to reject the null for their own work. Consequences: the report's claims become statistical (effect sizes, variance explained, confidence bounds), the run-to-run noise of agent runs becomes the floor the operator effect must clear, and the replay experiment becomes a power-analysis problem: how many runs before the rejection is honest. The second research round goes deeper on exactly that machinery.
## 2026-08-13 — the methodology round landed, and it changes three product requirements

The synthesis lives as zettel notes jv72 (the design and the numbers) and q4rd (the claim shape and the credibility stack), linked to the first-round notes. Three findings become product requirements. First, the replay runner needs a calibration mode: same-condition repeats that measure this pipeline's own noise floor before any expert-versus-stripped gap gets interpreted, because published noise numbers (2-6pp SD per run for agentic pipelines) do not transfer. Second, the capture layer gains an integrity requirement beyond git: a turn-level hash chain with session Merkle roots and an external timestamp anchor, because git timestamps are locally spoofable and the credibility stack needs tamper evidence a skeptic accepts. Third, the report's claim language becomes statistical: a shrunk effect size with an interval, a specification-curve sweep across scoring variants, a reported placebo arm, and a refusal to present anything below the measured noise floor as positive. The design itself: alternating treatments across matched independent task instances in three or more categories, never both conditions on one instance, about 15 matched pairs as the floor for a large effect, and two to three repeats per arm as the minimum. The open problem that stays ours: task-selection bias at n=1, mitigated but not eliminated by the placebo arm.
## 2026-08-13 — the two nulls, the third arm, and the tier principle

The user corrected an overstatement: the layperson replays are a real comparison arm, so the design is a controlled within-person experiment, and the n=1 identification problem does not touch the core claim. The precise split: Null A (expertise explains no variance) gets a fair test from expert-versus-layperson across matched task instances; Null B (this operator is fungible among experts, the unique-to-me reading) needs an expert reference class, and that is where n=1 bites. The design extension for Null B: a third arm in the intervention ladder, a generic-expert-persona driver between the layperson and the operator, with the caveat that an LLM expert persona is a proxy for the reference class. The task-selection confound stays regardless, mitigated by the placebo arm. New design principle from the user's rigor point: claims carry their tier. The product spans descriptive (the record), correlational (attribution), and experimental (replay) tiers, each governed by its own field's methodology, and every number in any report names the tier that produced it and never borrows a stronger tier's credibility.
